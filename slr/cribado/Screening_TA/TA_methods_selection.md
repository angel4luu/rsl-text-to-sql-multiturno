# Selection process - title/abstract screening

Records retrieved from ACL Anthology (n = 133), ACM DL (n = 146), arXiv (n = 253), IEEE Xplore (n = 368), Scopus (n = 557) were combined and de-duplicated with the sr-screener skill (version 1.0.0) of the open-source Academic Research Skills suite (https://github.com/Imbad0202/academic-research-skills), running in Claude Code, which matches PMIDs, DOIs and normalised titles with publication year (plus or minus one year); 416 duplicates were removed, leaving 1041 unique records. Titles and abstracts were screened against the pre-specified eligibility criteria of the review protocol. Each record was assessed independently by two AI reviewers (both Claude Sonnet 5.5 (claude-sonnet-5-5), prompted with different expert personas) that were given the same written protocol and decision rules and did not see each other's decisions. The reviewer agents could only read the record file they were given (no web access). Disagreements between advancing (include or unclear) and excluding a record (32 records) were resolved by a third AI reviewer (Claude Opus 5.5 (claude-opus-5-5)) that re-read the record. The two reviewers agreed on advance-versus-exclude decisions for 96.9% of records (Cohen's kappa = 0.938; prevalence-adjusted bias-adjusted kappa = 0.939). Before the full run, the AI screening was piloted on 60 records that the review team had labelled independently; the AI excluded 0 of the 28 records the team advanced.
